The challenge

A nightly CSV import couldn’t keep up with three warehouses

Northwind sells across their Shopify Plus store, a wholesale portal and two marketplaces. Stock levels lived in NetSuite and were pushed to Shopify once a night with a CSV import.

During busy weeks, products sold out in the warehouse but stayed available online for up to 20 hours. The team spent hours every Monday cancelling oversold orders and apologising to customers.

  • {{ icon('alert', 18) }}Oversold orders every week during peak periods
  • {{ icon('alert', 18) }}Around 12 hours a week of manual fixes across ops and support
  • {{ icon('alert', 18) }}No visibility when the import failed
Our approach

Map the real workflow before writing code

We started with a one-week technical review: how orders, returns and transfers actually moved between systems, where the data was owned, and what had to stay manual. We agreed a written scope with fixed milestones and a staging store to test against real data.

  1. 1
    Technical review & written scopeData owners, edge cases and success measures agreed up front.
  2. 2
    Event-driven sync on stagingWeekly previews with the ops team using real orders.
  3. 3
    Gradual rolloutOne warehouse at a time, with the old import kept as a fallback.
The solution

A custom app that keeps every channel in sync

We built a private Shopify app that listens to Shopify and NetSuite events, updates stock within two minutes and retries automatically when either side is unavailable. A simple dashboard shows sync health, and the team gets a Slack alert if anything needs attention.
